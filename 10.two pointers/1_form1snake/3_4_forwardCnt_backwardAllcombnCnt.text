In the context of the problem **"Number of Substrings Containing All Three Characters"**, the answer is **no**, you cannot simply use:

ans += head - tail + 1

The reason lies in exactly *what* you are counting. In sliding window problems, these two formulas count fundamentally different things.

-----------------------------------------

1. ans += n - head  (Counting Forward)

-----------------------------------------

This formula is used when you want to count substrings that **contain at least** a certain set of characters.

Logic:
Once the window [tail ... head] is valid, any character you add to the right side will keep it valid.

Example:
In the string "abcabc", if your window is "abc" (indices 0 to 2), then the following substrings are all valid:

abc
abca
abcab
abcabc

There are (n - head) such substrings starting at index `tail`.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------



2. ans += head - tail + 1  (Counting Backward)

---

This formula is used when you want to count **all possible substrings** within the current window. It is commonly used for problems like:

"Count substrings with at most K distinct characters."

Logic:
This adds the number of new substrings that *end* at the current `head`.

Example:
If your window is "abc", this formula adds 3 substrings:

"c"
"bc"
"abc"

The problem:
In this specific question, "c" and "bc" are NOT valid because they do not contain 'a' and 'b'. Using this formula would incorrectly count invalid substrings.

---

## Comparison

Formula:        ans += n - head
Counts:         Substrings starting at `tail` and ending at or after `head`
Use case:       "At least" problems (must contain A, B, and C)

Formula:        ans += head - tail + 1
Counts:         Substrings ending at `head` and starting at or after `tail`
Use case:       "At most" problems (e.g., at most K distinct characters)

---

## Summary

If you use:

ans += head - tail + 1

in an "at least" problem, you will count every small substring inside your window, including invalid ones like "a" or "ab" that do not meet the requirement.

To use backward counting for an "at least" problem, you would need extra logic to subtract invalid substrings — which makes the solution more complex.

That is why using:

ans += n - head

is the correct and simpler approach for this problem.
